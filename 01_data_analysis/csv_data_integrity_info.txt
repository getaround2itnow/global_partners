footnote1:

1 instance of lineitem_id, item_category, and item_name all belong 
to the same record with the following details that are available:

APP_NAME		Alton Fresh

RESTAURANT_ID		5e7e35ed902ad5ac017b242b

CREATION_TIME_UTC	2021-12-07T16:23:30.917Z

ORDER_ID		61af8a82ffd125453e62ae37

USER_ID			609d681462e498b356e72a6d

IS_LOYALTY		FALSE

CURRENCY		USD

ITEM_PRICE		4:39

QUANTITY		0

There are 24 other records available for this USER_ID, ORDER_ID combo where the order actually went through for a non-zero amount of items.
Though it's only a very small part of the total dataset, the impact of the anomaly is unknown, so making a note of it for awareness.

footnote2:

Investigation shows that the 28 broken reference rows have 15 unique ORDER_ID + LINEITEM_ID combinations & 14 unique ORDER_IDs.  There's no 
parent orders for this and options for the records are normal as far as structure and context.  Could be due to a matter related to export or synchronization, or orders not being properly input to the order_items dataset.  Making a note of the irregularity for future awareness.

footnote3:

A total of 616 records have 2 or more duplicates, creating a total of 2,915.  Investigation found all 6 columns were duplicated, making them completely identical.

Possible causes of the duplicates:

1. Creation from an upstream export
2. The same records could have been appended more than once by an ETL process.
3. Intentional repeat but failure to supply a unique identifier.
4. A source system emitting more than 1 option events.
5. Another anomaly in the process of data generation.

Instead of deleting the duplicated columns, I'm keeping the file "csv_data_integrity_report.xlsx " available for future reference and also to ensure that persons with a need to know can be aware.
